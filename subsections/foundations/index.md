# Foundations

Every protocol in this guide quietly assumes a handful of small skills: running a gel, knowing how much DNA is in a tube, cleaning up a reaction, and doing the arithmetic that turns a recipe into volumes you can actually pipette. None of them belong to any single topic, which is why they get their own chapter instead of being tucked into cloning or cell culture.

If you have ever read a protocol that said "add 50 ng of DNA" and wondered how you were supposed to know what that looks like, this chapter is for you. Nobody is born knowing this stuff, and it is much less intimidating once someone walks you through it.

## Where you will meet these skills

You do not have to read this chapter first. You will run into these ideas in the other chapters, and I will link back here instead of explaining them over and over:

- The [PCR protocol](/protocols/pcr.md) ends by checking the product on an agarose gel.
- The [ligation](/protocols/t4_ligase.md) and [Golden Gate](/protocols/golden_gate.md) protocols specify DNA by mass and by moles, which means you need a way to measure and convert.
- The [cloning chapter](/subsections/cloning/index.md) points to kits for cleaning up reactions and purifying DNA from a gel.

## What this chapter will cover

I am still writing this chapter, so for now this is a map of where it is headed. Each of these will get its own page:

- **Gel electrophoresis**: separating DNA by size and reading what you see.
- **DNA quantification**: measuring how much DNA you have.
- **Cleanup**: purifying DNA after a PCR, a digest, or a gel.
- **Solution math**: concentrations, dilutions, and converting a recipe into volumes.

If there is a skill you wish someone had explained to you in your first week, I would love to hear about it. Open an [issue](https://github.com/samholtzen/labmanual/issues) and we can add it to the list.
